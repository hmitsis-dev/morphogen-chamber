what this is

this is being drawn, live, by real ICMP echo packets — ordinary-looking pings — flowing between two servers right now. nothing here is fake or pre-rendered.

how

one service sends a normal-looking ping roughly a dozen times a second. hidden in the data section, after the ping header, is a small binary frame: two magic bytes, a sequence number, a timestamp, and four numbers. the other service sniffs every echo request that reaches it, checks for the magic bytes, decodes the frame, and pushes it to this page over a websocket.

  • huewhich color the particles and grid drift toward
  • energyparticle brightness, size, and spawn rate
  • speedhow fast the flow field and grid move
  • bursttriggers the expanding ring you'll see occasionally

is any of this secure?

there's nothing sensitive to protect here — the payload is just the four numbers above, nothing personal or secret. but to be honest about the mechanism: ICMP has no built-in authentication. the receiver only accepts packets whose source IP matches the sender it's configured for, but source IPs can be spoofed on networks that don't filter outbound traffic, so in principle someone could forge packets and inject their own values into this page. it doesn't matter here — there's nothing to steal and nothing that actually breaks — but it's a disguise, not a security boundary.
